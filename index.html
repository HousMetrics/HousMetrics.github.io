About the project

This project outlines how causal inference differs from simple correlation and why hidden factors can distort interpretation.

Correlation

Correlation is not causation

Observed patterns may appear meaningful while actually reflecting a shared driver.

Confounding

Hidden variables matter

A confounder can cause both variables to move together and produce misleading results.

Modeling

Better estimation

By adjusting for the right variables, we can estimate effect sizes more faithfully.

Example framework

A simple causal setup can be represented visually and then modeled in code. 

X = ice_cream_sales.reshape(-1, 1)
y = shark_attacks
model = LinearRegression()
model.fit(X, y)

print("Intercept:", model.intercept_)
print("Slope:", model.coef_[0])

Key insight

The relationship between two variables may look strong while the real causal effect is driven by an omitted factor — a classic case of confounding bias.

In the example discussed in this project, the apparent effect of ice cream sales on shark attacks was misleading because both are influenced by summer conditions. Once the confounder is included, the true relationship becomes much clearer and far less dramatic.
